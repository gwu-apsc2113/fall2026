# Welcome to APSC 2113: Engineering Analysis I [Fall 2026]
{: .fs-9 }

**Welcome, and congratulations on making it to APSC 2113! Whether you're returning to campus with renewed energy or stepping into engineering analysis for the first time, I'm glad you are here.**

From the way a bridge sways in the wind, to how current flows through a circuit, to the cooling of a hot engine block, ODEs are the mathematical language that lets us describe and predict how real systems change over time. By the end of this semester, you'll not only know how to solve these equations — you'll understand why they matter and how to apply them to problems you'll encounter throughout your engineering career. </strong></p>

**Here's a bit of what's ahead:** 

- First and second-order ODEs — the building blocks for modeling motion, growth, decay, and vibration
- Modeling physical systems — translating real engineering scenarios (circuits, mechanical systems, heat transfer) into mathematical form
- Analytical and numerical solution methods — including Laplace transforms and computational approaches
- Applications across disciplines — mechanical, electrical, civil, and beyond

I know differential equations can feel intimidating at first, but I promise: with steady practice, curiosity, and a willingness to ask questions, this material will start to click — and you'll begin to see it everywhere, in nearly every system around you.

This is a space for learning together. Don't hesitate to reach out, come to office hours, or collaborate with classmates. We're all here to help each other succeed.

Looking forward to a great semester with you.
Welcome aboard!

## Audience and credit level
- The course is designed for sophomore-level undergraduate students as a 3-credit course.

## Workload
- This course will have 150 minutes of lecture time per week. The course requires an average of 2 to 4 hours per week on average for homework assignments or project-based deliverables.

## Textbook
- **Recommended textbook:**
Elementary Differential Equations with Boundary Value Problems by C. Henry Edwards and David E. Penney (5th or 6th Edition). Either edition will work well for our purposes. 
- **Suggested reference but not required for this course:** 
Differential Equations and Linear Algebra, by Gilbert Strang. Note that I love Gilbert Strang and his teaching approach. So, I kept this book as a standard reference for myself.

## Lectures
-	We will meet two times a week for lectures, problem solving, and group work.
-	I have not pre-filmed lectures for this course.


## Little Quizzes (LQs), Regular Quizzes (RQs), and HomeWorks (HWs)
-	You will receive up to 8 LQs during the course
    - LQs are 5-7 mins in duration 
    - LQs designed to test concepts
    - LQs can be done in collaboration with up to three students in class
-	You will receive up to 5 RQs during the course
    - RQs are up to 15 minutes in duration
    - RQs are designed to test problem-solving skills
    - RQs are to be completed individually in class.
-	You will receive up to 5 homework assignments, approximately every 3 weeks.
    - The assignments are longer, and you are expected to spend about 3 weeks on each assignment.
    - I recommend starting early so you have time to complete the assignments and get help if needed.
- Homework assignments are study tools, and you may use any resource to complete them.

## Exams
- Two exams (Exam 1 and Exam 2) will be given (check the dates now!).
- They will be based on lectures, assigned reading, homework, and classwork.
- They will be given in class and will be open-note, closed-book, individual work.
- Open notes will include notes provided by the instructor during the course and any additional material students may generate.
  - The notes must be strictly handwritten or prints of the tablet-generated, handwritten notes for the exams
  - Tablets, laptops and any handheld devices will not be allowed during exams.
  - If you use a tablet and stylus, that is fine, but typing, copying, and pasting, or screenshotting the textbook will not be considered for a grade.
  - If you need an accommodation for the handwritten policy, please contact me, and we will find a solution for you
  - A full week’s notice is required to request an alternative exam date, except in cases of documented illness.
  - Exams must be completed using paper and pen/pencil only.
  - Each exam is worth 30 points and is not strictly cumulative. However, concepts will build on one another, so knowledge of prior topics may still be required.
